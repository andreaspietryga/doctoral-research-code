# Safety Investment and Market Introduction of Automated Vehicles:
# An Analysis of Endogenous Training Effects

## Overview

This directory contains the Wolfram Mathematica implementations supporting the
second paper of my doctoral dissertation in Computational Economics.

The project develops a two-stage framework that links the technological learning
process of an autonomous vehicle with dynamic investment and market-introduction
decisions.

In the first stage, reinforcement learning is used to simulate the learning
behavior of an autonomous vehicle in a stylized driving environment. The
resulting performance observations are used to characterize the relationship
between training effort and operational performance.

In the second stage, these performance data serve as inputs to a dynamic
investment problem. A predetermined open-loop investment strategy is compared
with adaptive, state-dependent investment approaches based on the Dyna-2
reinforcement learning algorithm.

The overall framework combines technological learning, simulation, and economic
decision-making in order to study investment under heterogeneous and evolving
performance.

## Research Questions

The project investigates:

- how autonomous-vehicle performance evolves through learning;
- how learning affects optimal training and safety investment;
- how investment decisions change when learning trajectories are heterogeneous
  and volatile;
- how predetermined open-loop investment differs from adaptive state-dependent
  investment;
- and whether adaptive investment strategies improve economic performance.

## Computational Framework

The project consists of two connected stages.

### Stage 1: Autonomous-Vehicle Learning

A TD(0)-Search-based learning algorithm is used to simulate an autonomous
vehicle operating in a stylized road environment.

The simulations generate realized performance observations including:

- accidents,
- inappropriate driving,
- and driving steps.

These observations are subsequently used to construct the performance
distributions required by the economic investment models.

### Stage 2: Dynamic Investment Decisions

The simulated performance information is incorporated into a finite-horizon
investment problem.

Two broad approaches are studied:

1. an **open-loop investment strategy**, in which investment decisions are
   determined without adapting future actions to subsequently realized learning
   states; and

2. **adaptive Dyna-2-based investment strategies**, in which investment
   decisions can respond to the current state of the learning process.

The Dyna-2 experiments consider different informational settings, including
cases with and without previously acquired knowledge.

## Files

### `td0-search-learning-simulation.nb`

Simulates the autonomous vehicle with learning enabled.

The notebook generates performance outcomes that characterize how the vehicle's
behavior changes through training.

### `td0-search-non-learning-baseline.nb`

Simulates the corresponding environment without learning.

This provides a benchmark for assessing the effect of the learning process on
vehicle performance.

### `realized-performance-data.nb`

Contains the realized performance observations generated by the simulation
experiments.

The stored observations include:

- accidents,
- inappropriate driving,
- and driving steps.

These observations are used to construct the performance distributions that
serve as inputs to the subsequent investment models.

This notebook contains stored simulation results rather than additional model
code.

### `open-loop-investment-strategy.nb`

Implements the finite-horizon investment problem under an open-loop strategy.

The investment decisions are based on the available performance information but
do not adapt to subsequently realized learning states.

### `dyna2-baseline.nb`

Contains the baseline implementation of the Dyna-2 investment framework used to
study adaptive, state-dependent investment decisions.

### `dyna2-zero-knowledge.nb`

Implements a Dyna-2 experiment in which the investment and learning process
starts without previously acquired knowledge.

### `dyna2-with-prior-knowledge.nb`

Implements a Dyna-2 experiment in which previously acquired information is
available at the beginning of the decision process.

### `dyna2-results-analysis.nb`

Contains the evaluation and comparison of outcomes generated by the Dyna-2
investment experiments.

### `dyna2-robustness-analysis.nb`

Contains additional robustness analyses of the Dyna-2 results.

## Computational Workflow

The general workflow is:

```text
TD(0)-Search learning simulations
                |
                v
     Realized performance data
                |
                v
   Performance distributions
                |
        -----------------
        |               |
        v               v
 Open-loop model    Dyna-2 models
                        |
                        v
              Results and robustness
```

The TD(0)-Search simulations generate the underlying performance observations.
These observations characterize the learning process and provide the empirical
inputs for the investment models.

The open-loop and Dyna-2 approaches then use this performance information to
study different investment strategies.

The individual Dyna-2 notebooks represent alternative experimental
configurations and do not necessarily have to be executed sequentially.

## Main Results

The computational analysis shows that explicitly modeling the technological
learning process can materially affect optimal investment decisions.

In particular:

- autonomous-vehicle learning generates substantial performance improvements,
  but the learning process is heterogeneous and does not follow a smooth
  deterministic trajectory;

- realized accidents, inappropriate driving, and driving steps vary across
  learning trajectories;

- optimal investment decisions depend on the state of technological learning;

- additional investment can become unattractive as the end of the finite
  planning horizon approaches;

- adaptive state-dependent investment can respond to realized learning
  trajectories in a way that a predetermined open-loop strategy cannot;

- and the Dyna-2-based adaptive strategy outperforms the open-loop benchmark in
  the analyzed simulations.

## Software Requirements

All notebooks in this directory have been tested successfully with:

**Wolfram Mathematica 14**

## Running the Notebooks

For computational notebooks:

1. Open the desired `.nb` file in Wolfram Mathematica.
2. Start with a clean Mathematica kernel.
3. Evaluate the notebook from top to bottom.
4. Model parameters are defined within the respective notebook.
5. Depending on the simulation or optimization task, execution time may vary
   with hardware and parameter settings.

`realized-performance-data.nb` primarily contains stored simulation outcomes
rather than an additional computational model.

Because the Dyna-2 notebooks represent different experimental configurations,
they can be examined and executed independently where appropriate.
